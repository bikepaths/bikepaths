Modern science operates on a set of shared ideas that guide lab trials and university study. Over the past two centuries, these basic premises hardened into a rigid creed known as matter-first thought. While this worldview delivered great gains in chemical plants, electric grids, and medical tools, it also built sharp walls around open human thought. Looking at the ten core dogmas of standard science shows how unquestioned habits narrow our view of life, mind, and the physical universe.

Academic centers often treat these ten ideas as settled facts rather than working guesses. Young thinkers learn to read lab data through a narrow lens. They set aside field observations that clash with textbook claims. When strange findings appear in quantum tests or mental studies, established voices often wave them away as testing flaws or silly tales. Reopening these ten premises to honest questioning helps bring back the free search that sparked the early rise of science.

Such dogmas govern how funding boards hand out research grants across major universities. Scholars who question established models frequently find their work shut out of mainstream journals. Graduate students learn to avoid controversial frontiers to protect their future career prospects. When intellectual gatekeepers penalize original dissent, scientific discovery slows down behind walls of comfortable agreement.

**Clockwork Models of Nature and the Puzzle of Inner Life**

The first core dogma claims that nature runs like a machine, with living creatures acting as complex biological clockwork. Under this view, animals and human beings possess no genuine agency or inward aim. They move through life as gene-driven robots. Closely tied to this machine premise is the second belief that all physical matter lacks inner life or feeling. Rocks, water drops, and distant suns are assumed to be devoid of awareness, leaving human thought as a weird evolutionary fluke.

This view leads to the seventh claim, which insists that inner thought exists only inside the physical brain. Materialist theorists claim that thoughts, dreams, and personal memories are nothing more than sparks firing through wet brain cells. The eighth dogma follows along by asserting that human memories are stored as tiny physical marks that vanish at bodily death. By locking mind inside skull bone, standard science cannot explain how feeling arises from dead matter. This failure creates a famous puzzle known as the hard problem of mind.

**Cosmic Laws and the Premise of Random Evolution**

The third dogma holds that the total sum of matter and energy in the universe stays forever fixed. The only exception occurred at the sudden birth of the Big Bang, when everything sprang from empty space. Next sits the fourth dogma, which claims that the basic laws of nature are fixed and eternal. Theorists assert that physical rules stay the same from the cosmic dawn to the present hour. This view overlooks the idea that cosmic habits might evolve over time alongside the expanding cosmos, acting more like growing habits than rigid stone laws.

In life sciences, the fifth dogma states that evolutionary growth moves forward without aim. Growth is driven only by random gene errors and environmental sorting. Nature is treated as without purpose, stripping living things of direction or meaning. This outlook is backed by the sixth claim, which insists that biological inheritance travels only through physical strings of genetic code. By looking past field effects and cellular memory, standard biology treats living creatures as passive software shaped by fixed chemical chains.

**Taboos Around Odd Perceptions and Corporate Medicine**

The ninth dogma dismisses unexplained mental events, like mind-to-mind sensing and distant knowing, as empty illusions. Under matter-first theory, which requires all mind to remain trapped inside skull tissue, reports of shared feeling or sudden foresight are thrown out without trial. University researchers who look into these cognitive riddles risk lost grants and harsh social mockery from peers. This institutional taboo stops science from studying subtle aspects of human awareness that lie beyond coarse physical tools.

The tenth dogma insists that drug-based medicine represents the only sound way to heal the human body. Mainstream doctors often view the living body as an intricate chemical engine that can be repaired only by synthetic pills or surgical knives. While emergency surgery saves lives during acute crises, this narrow view frequently downplays the deep bond between human hope, neighbor support, and bodily healing. Older healing traditions with hundreds of years of practical trial are cast aside as primitive folklore.

Rigid trust in these ten dogmas turns scientific study from an open quest into a closed belief system. Genuine science requires steady questioning, humble eyes, and willingness to follow real facts wherever they travel. When scholars treat working tools as unquestioned truths, intellectual growth halts behind dogma. Rethinking these ten matter-first dogmas opens the door to a richer grasp of nature, human awareness, and the living world.
